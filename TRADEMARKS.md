## Why the policy exists

The MPL-2.0 license grants a right to the code. It grants no right to a trademark. Section 2.3 of the
MPL states this rule.

The rule protects the reader, not the copyright holder. A reader who opens a site under the name
**Fireplace of Despair** must reach the organization that this repository describes. A fork under the
same name breaks that promise. The reader cannot tell the two apart, and a statement on the fork
lands on the reputation of the original.

## The written content

This project is a website. The pages carry the lore, the projects, the articles, and the press
entries of Fireplace of Despair. That text is not code, and the license does not cover it. Neither do
the pictures and the fonts that the repository carries or points to.

The database rows, the seed data, and the markdown in `StainlessInfrastructure/.import/` follow the
same rule. Read [LICENSING.md](LICENSING.md).

## What you may do without permission

1. Say that your site builds on TLD-15, or works with it.
2. Name TLD-15 or Fireplace of Despair in an article, a review, a talk, or a comparison.
3. Keep the name inside the source code, the license headers, and the copyright notices of a fork.
   Section 3.4 of the MPL asks you to keep these notices. Section 3.1 carries the same duty into a
   modified version.
4. Run an unchanged build for yourself, for your company, or for your family.
5. Run a site for other people, and tell them which software you run.

## What needs written permission

1. Publish a fork under the name **Fireplace of Despair**, under the name **TLD-15**, or under a name
   that a reader confuses with either.
2. Publish a fork with the Fireplace of Despair logo or icon.
3. Publish a fork that keeps the lore, the articles, the press entries, or the division names.
4. Use a mark in a domain name, a company name, or a name of a paid service.
5. Say or imply that Shevtsov Stanislav endorses your fork.

## The rule for a fork

**Rename your fork and replace its content before you publish it.** Change four things.

1. The organization name that the pages show.
2. The logo, the icon, and the pictures.
3. The written content: the lore, the projects, the articles, and the press entries.
4. The division names, and the reference rows that carry them.

Keep the copyright headers and the license notices. The MPL asks for that, and this policy does not
change it.

A correct sentence for a fork reads like this:

> Ember Hall is a fork of TLD-15. Shevtsov Stanislav does not endorse Ember Hall.
